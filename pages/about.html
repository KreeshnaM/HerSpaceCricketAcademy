Our story

It started because
nobody else was doing it.

Girls' cricket in Kenya deserves more room to grow. We're trying to create that space.

Women's cricket in Kenya isn't growing the way we'd like it to. When Tiara, a former captain of the Kenyan national team, started this academy, it came from a frustration she knew well: the lack of opportunities for girls to play and develop. The girls who make it to the national team have largely grown up playing alongside boys and men. We wanted younger girls to have a different experience — a place where they could learn the game alongside other girls, push each other to improve, and see what was possible for themselves.

But the academy has become about more than cricket. Our girls come from different backgrounds, each with their own challenges outside the game. Here, they have a team to belong to, a place where they can feel safe, build confidence, and grow as individuals as much as players.

Nobody here is paid. Our coaches are parents, former players, teachers and older girls who once stood where our youngest players stand today. We make do with donated kit, borrowed resources and a lot of goodwill. It's not always easy, but the girls keep showing up, and so do we.

What we're for

Three things we
actually promise.

We are careful about what we claim, because girls notice when a place says one thing and does another.

She will get better

Real coaching, not childminding with a bat. Whatever level she starts at, she will leave the season better than she arrived.

She will be looked after

Checked, trained adults. Clear rules. Somebody whose job it is to notice if she has gone quiet.

She will belong somewhere

A team that knows her name and expects her on Saturday. For some girls that turns out to be the whole point.

How volunteering works

Nobody here
gets paid.

That is not a funding problem we are trying to solve. It is how the place works, and it changes the atmosphere.

Every coach, scorer, driver and kit-washer is here because they chose to be on a Saturday morning. Subscriptions cover pitch hire, balls, insurance and the kit we lend out - nothing else.

It also means we are never more than a few people away from not being able to run. If you have ever thought about helping, that is the honest reason to.
