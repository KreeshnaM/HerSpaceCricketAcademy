Our story

It began with a simple belief: every girl belongs in the game.

Women's cricket in Kenya is not growing as quickly as it should. When Tiara, a former captain of the Kenyan national team, founded this academy, she was responding to a frustration she knew well: girls have too few opportunities to play and develop. Most girls who reach the national team have grown up playing alongside boys and men. We wanted younger girls to have a different experience: a place to learn the game alongside other girls, push each other to improve, and see what is possible for themselves.

But the academy has become about more than cricket. Our girls come from different backgrounds, and many face challenges outside the game. Here, they have a team to belong to and a safe place to build confidence, growing as individuals as much as players.

Nobody here is paid. Our coaches are parents, former players, teachers and older girls who once stood where our youngest players stand today. We make do with donated kit, borrowed resources and a great deal of goodwill. It is not always easy, but the girls keep showing up, and so do we.

Safeguarding

Safeguarding is at the heart of our work.

Every girl has the right to be safe, respected and heard. Safeguarding is not an add-on at HerSpace. It shapes how we run every session.

Safe people

Coaches and volunteers are carefully selected and agree to our code of conduct.

Safe sessions

Training is open, supervised and group-based, with clear expectations for behaviour.

Safe to speak up

Girls and families know who to talk to, and every concern is taken seriously and acted on.

What we're for

Three things we
actually promise.

We are careful about what we claim, because girls notice when a place says one thing and does another.

She will get better

Real coaching, not childminding with a bat. Whatever level she starts at, she will leave the season better than she arrived.

She will be looked after

Checked, trained adults. Clear rules. Somebody whose job it is to notice if she has gone quiet.

She will belong somewhere

A team that knows her name and expects her on Saturday. For some girls that turns out to be the whole point.

Volunteering

That is not a funding problem we are trying to solve. It is simply how the academy works, and it shapes the atmosphere. Every coach, scorer, driver and kit washer is here because they chose to be, on a Saturday morning. Subscriptions cover pitch hire, balls, insurance and the kit we lend out, and nothing else.

It also means we are never more than a few people away from being unable to run. If you have ever thought about helping, that is the honest reason to.

Our community

It is bigger than
the eleven playing.

Parents who stay and end up scoring. Grandparents on the boundary in deckchairs. Older players who came through the academy and now coach the group they were once in.

We share a ground with a cricket club, work with three local schools, and come together with parents and our partners to reach girls who would never otherwise hear about us.
