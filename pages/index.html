Who we are

A girls' cricket academy that runs on borrowed time, donated kit and a lot of goodwill — and it works.

Nobody here is paid. Our coaches are parents, former players, teachers and older girls who came through the academy themselves. What we offer is simple: a proper place for girls to learn cricket, get better at it, and be part of a team that wants them there.

Some of our players will go on to play county cricket. Most won't, and that has never been the point. They all leave knowing they can pick something hard and get good at it.

How the academy started

Why cricket?

It teaches them things
we never planned to teach.

Cricket is a slow game with a lot of waiting and a lot of responsibility. It turns out that is very good for you.

  • 01

    Confidence

    Facing a ball in front of everyone is frightening the first time. After a few weeks she stops flinching, and she notices.

  • 02

    Friendship

    Eleven girls standing in a field for two hours will end up friends. A lot of them arrive not knowing anybody.

  • 03

    Discipline

    You cannot rush a cover drive. Practising the same thing until it works is a skill, and it transfers to everything else.

  • 04

    Opportunity

    Girls' cricket still isn't offered everywhere. Where it isn't, we would rather be the ones who ran it than waited for it.

Our academy

What actually
happens here.

Four things, every week, in roughly this order of importance.

Training

Weekly sessions split by experience, so nobody is bored and nobody is out of their depth.

  • Brand-new beginners group
  • Developing players
  • Match squad practice

Coaching

Volunteer coaches, properly qualified and properly checked. Small groups, so she gets spoken to by name.

  • Batting, bowling and fielding basics
  • Match awareness and captaincy
  • Female coaches at every session

Games

Real matches against real opposition, because practice on its own gets dull and you learn more in one game than a month of drills.

  • Friendlies and festival days
  • League fixtures for the older groups
  • Everybody gets a game, not just the best

Community

Girls encouraging each other loudly and without embarrassment. It is the thing visitors comment on first.

  • Older players mentoring younger ones
  • Families welcome to stay and watch
  • End-of-season presentation night

Every week, rain allowing
